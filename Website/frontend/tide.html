The tidal Thames, Teddington to the sea

Once it's in the river, it doesn't go straight out.

The Thames through London is tidal. A spill is carried up the river, then back down, twice a day, moving a little closer to the sea each time.

What we can't tell you

We know when each spill began, where, and what the tide was doing. We don't know how much came out, and we can't follow the water itself.

Tide times are worked out from tide data for the Thames estuary and checked against the Silvertown gauge. They match the gauge about 96% of the time.
