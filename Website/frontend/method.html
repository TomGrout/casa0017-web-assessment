Method & sources

How Rain To Spill collects its data, where every number comes from, and the research behind the project.

How it works

  1. 1

    Check the outfalls

    Every 15 minutes our server asks Thames Water which storm overflows on the River Lea, Bow Creek and the Thames are spilling sewage.

  2. 2

    Measure rain, river and tide

    At the same time it collects rainfall from the Wanstead Park rain gauge, the river level at Lea Bridge and the tide level at Silvertown from the Environment Agency.

  3. 3

    Collect past spills

    Past spills come from Thames Water's monitors, published by the Environment Agency. The records start in January 2024 and give the time each spill started and stopped.

  4. 4

    Link spills to rain and tide

    For each spill we add up the rain that fell at that outfall in the 24 hours before it started, using Open-Meteo's weather history. We also work out whether the tide was rising or falling at that moment, from tide data for the Thames estuary that we checked against the Silvertown gauge.

Live data we use

These APIs (online services that send data to our server) keep the site up to date.

Thames Water

Open Data API: storm discharges

The list of storm overflow outfalls, where they are, and whether each one is spilling right now.

Used for: outfalls, live spills

data.thameswater.co.uk

Environment Agency

Real-time flood-monitoring API

Rainfall at Wanstead Park, river level at Lea Bridge, and tide level at Silvertown, measured every 15 minutes.

Used for: rain, river level, tide

environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring

Environment Agency

Flood warnings

Current flood alerts and warnings for Greater London.

Used for: flood alerts

environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring

Open-Meteo

Weather history and forecast APIs

Hourly rain at each outfall for past spills, plus the rain forecast and soil moisture, which tells us how wet the ground already is.

Used for: rain before each spill, wet catchment

open-meteo.com

Open-Meteo

Marine weather API

Hourly tide heights in the Thames estuary since 2024. We shift them by about two hours to match the Silvertown gauge, which gives the high and low tide times for past spills. They agree with the gauge about 96% of the time.

Used for: where it goes

open-meteo.com/en/docs/marine-weather-api

RainViewer

Weather radar API

The latest rain radar images, showing where it is raining right now.

Used for: rain radar

rainviewer.com

Historical data

Environment Agency

Event Duration Monitoring: start and stop data

The time each storm overflow in England started and stopped spilling, as reported by the water companies.

Used for: past spills

environment.data.gov.uk
