London's rivers

Where does the sewage go after it rains?

Into the nearest river, then down to the Thames. But the Thames is tidal, so twice a day it pushes back upstream, up rivers like the Lea toward Stratford.

Rain

over London

Rain and sewage share a pipe.

London's Victorian sewers carry both. When heavy rain fills them, the extra spills straight into the river.

Sometimes a shower is enough.

It doesn't take a storm. Plenty of London's spills follow a few hours of light rain.

Tide

on the Thames

Then the tide decides.

The Thames through London is tidal. Twice a day it pushes back upstream, and up creeks like Bow Creek. If an outfall spills while the tide is rising, the sewage goes upstream first.
